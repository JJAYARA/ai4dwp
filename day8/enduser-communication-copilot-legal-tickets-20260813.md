# Copilot Support — What's Happening & What To Do Next
**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Audience:** Legal Team — affected users  
**From:** DWP Endpoint Support

---

We have reviewed the recent Copilot support requests raised by members of the Legal team. Below is a plain-English explanation of what is happening for each issue and the steps you can take right now.

---

## "I asked Copilot to summarise a document and it said it doesn't have access"
**Who this affects:** Users who asked Copilot about a file they had not previously opened or been given access to.

**What's happening:**  
Copilot can only work with documents you already have permission to open yourself. If you heard about a file in a meeting but have never been given access to it, Copilot will not be able to read it — this is intentional and keeps your organisation's information secure.

**What to do next:**  
1. Try opening the file directly in SharePoint. If you see an "Access denied" message, you do not have permission to view it.  
2. Contact the document owner or your matter lead and ask them to share the file with you through the normal SharePoint sharing process.  
3. Once access has been granted, wait a few minutes and try Copilot again.

---

## "Copilot in Outlook can't find any of my emails"
**Who this affects:** Users who joined the organisation recently (within the last few days).

**What's happening:**  
When a new account is set up, Microsoft 365 needs a few days to index your mailbox before Copilot can search through it. This is normal and temporary — your emails are not lost. Copilot also needs a licence to be active on your account, which can take up to 24 hours to fully apply after it is assigned.

**What to do next:**  
1. Wait 24–72 hours from your account activation date and try again.  
2. If Copilot is still unavailable after 3 working days, raise a ticket with the IT helpdesk so they can confirm your licence has been correctly assigned.  
3. In the meantime, you can search for emails using the standard Outlook search bar, which works independently of Copilot.

---

## "Copilot showed me a document from a matter I'm not assigned to"
**Who this affects:** Users who were unexpectedly shown content from a matter or folder outside their current assignments.

**What's happening:**  
Copilot only ever shows you content you already have permission to access. If it surfaced this document, it means your account has inherited broader access to that area than intended — most likely through a SharePoint folder or site group that was not scoped correctly. This is a permissions configuration issue, not a Copilot error.

**What to do next:**  
1. Do not share or act on the content of the document you were not expecting to see.  
2. Report this to your IT helpdesk or information governance team immediately so they can review and correct the permissions on that matter folder.  
3. You do not need to take any further action yourself — the IT team will handle the correction.

---

## "Our whole team lost access to Copilot this morning"
**Who this affects:** All members of the Legal team who had working Copilot access last week but cannot use it today.

**What's happening:**  
A sudden loss of access for an entire team is almost always caused by a change to licences or account group settings — for example, a licence being removed or reassigned overnight, or a policy update that affected the team's entitlements. The IT team is investigating this now.

**What to do next:**  
1. There is nothing you need to do on your own device — restarting apps or signing out will not fix this.  
2. The IT helpdesk is already checking your licence assignments and will restore access as quickly as possible.  
3. If you need to complete urgent work that relies on Copilot, please contact the helpdesk directly and flag it as high priority so it can be escalated.  
4. We will send a follow-up message once access has been restored.

---

## "Copilot gives vague answers when I ask about our contract templates — it doesn't seem to read them"
**Who this affects:** Users working with a shared contract templates library who are getting generic responses instead of answers based on the actual document content.

**What's happening:**  
There are two likely reasons for this. Either the documents have not yet been fully indexed by Microsoft 365 Search (which can happen after a library is newly created or files are moved/uploaded in bulk), or the files carry a sensitivity label that prevents Copilot from processing their contents. In both cases, Copilot falls back to general knowledge rather than reading the specific document.

**What to do next:**  
1. Open one of the contract templates directly in Word Online and use the Copilot pane inside the document. If Copilot can answer your questions there, the issue is with search indexing rather than the document itself.  
2. If that also fails, check whether the documents have a sensitivity label applied (visible in the ribbon in Word). If they do, contact the IT helpdesk to confirm whether that label restricts AI processing.  
3. If the library was recently set up or had files added in bulk, wait 24–48 hours and try again — indexing usually completes within that window.  
4. If none of the above resolves it after 48 hours, raise a helpdesk ticket with the name of the library and an example file.
